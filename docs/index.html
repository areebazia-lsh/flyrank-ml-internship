03 · Methodology

Methodology

3.1 Research framing

The task is framed as a ranking and scoring problem for content-refresh prioritization. The objective is to identify pages that show combinations of observable signals that make them suitable for human review.

3.2 Feature engineering

Page-level signals were derived from the warehouse data, including:

  • 90-day impressions and clicks
  • Last-30-day and previous-30-day impressions and clicks
  • Average ranking position
  • Number of visible queries
  • Rare-query signals
  • Content age and days since update
  • Search volume and competition
  • Backlinks
  • Content length and structure

Additional directional signals were calculated from changes between the latest 30-day period and the previous 30-day period.

Signal Definition used
Stale At least 90 days since the last update
Meaningful visibility At least 100 impressions in the 90-day period
Declining At least 20% impression decline
Ranking deterioration Average position worsened by at least 5 positions
CTR opportunity Visible page with non-zero clicks and CTR at or below the 25th percentile

The observed CTR-opportunity threshold was approximately 0.001263.

3.3 Observed opportunity label

The refresh_opportunity label was constructed from combinations of the observable signals. A page was labeled as an opportunity when it satisfied at least one of the following conditions:

  1. Stale + meaningful visibility + impression decline
  2. Meaningful visibility + impression decline + ranking deterioration
  3. Stale + meaningful visibility + low CTR
Important: This is a defined observed opportunity condition, not a ground-truth measure of whether refreshing a page would succeed.

3.4 Transparent baseline

A transparent scoring baseline was created as an interpretable reference. The score combines:

  • 30% staleness
  • 25% visibility
  • 20% impression decline
  • 15% ranking position
  • 10% CTR opportunity

Components were normalized or capped to reduce the influence of extreme observations.

3.5 Machine-learning model

A Random Forest classifier was trained using 20 numeric features. Missing numeric values were median-imputed using statistics fitted only on the training data.

05 · Limitations

Limitations and Honest Framing

  1. The available warehouse data contain one fixed observation window rather than a before-and-after refresh experiment. The analysis cannot determine whether a future content refresh would improve impressions, clicks, CTR, or ranking position.
  2. The opportunity label is constructed from observable rules. Because several of the same signals used to construct the label are also used as model features, the high Precision@K partly reflects the model's ability to recover the predefined opportunity pattern.
  3. The client-grouped split prevents pages from the same client appearing in both training and testing. However, only 11 clients were held out, so performance may differ on a larger or different client population.
  4. Search-performance signals alone do not capture editorial quality, business priorities, conversion value, brand considerations, topical authority, or human judgment.
  5. The ranking should be treated as a decision-support system for prioritizing human review, not as an automated instruction that every high-scoring page should be refreshed.
Scope of claims: No causal claims are made about Google's ranking system or about the effect of content refreshes.
06 · Recommendations

Ranked Recommendations

The final system produces a ranked list of anonymized content pages using the Random Forest opportunity score. The ranking is intended to help a content team decide which pages deserve human review first.

  1. Refresh / review
    Prioritize pages showing strong combinations of decline, staleness, ranking deterioration, and meaningful visibility.
  2. Review CTR opportunity
    Examine pages with meaningful visibility but relatively weak observed CTR.
  3. Review for refresh
    Consider stale pages with additional weaker performance signals.
  4. Monitor
    Keep pages with limited evidence of an immediate opportunity under observation rather than automatically changing them.
Priority Recommended action Typical supporting signals
High Refresh / review Decline, stale content, worsening position, meaningful visibility
Medium Review CTR opportunity Meaningful visibility with relatively weak CTR
Medium Review for refresh Staleness combined with weaker performance signals
Low Monitor Limited evidence of an immediate opportunity

The ranked output should be treated as a review queue. Human review remains necessary before taking action because the model does not observe all editorial or business context.
